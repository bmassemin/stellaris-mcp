# Economy Guide (Stellaris 4.3 Cetus)

## Resource Priority
Energy > Minerals > Food > Alloys > Consumer Goods > Research > Unity

**Never go negative on energy** -- it cascades into other deficits. Minerals are the bottleneck feeding alloys, consumer goods, districts, and buildings.

## Early Game (First 30 Years)

1. Build districts reactively -- only when pops are about to grow into unemployment. Empty jobs cost upkeep for zero return.
2. Food districts first (biological empires need pop growth), then mining, then energy to stay positive.
3. Don't spam alloy production early unless doing a military rush -- the mineral cost is heavy.
4. Use the market: sell excess food/minerals, buy what you need. Stay within the no-price-change threshold (~42 energy/minerals/food, ~21 CG, ~10 alloys per month).

## District Specialization System

Each planet has:
- **1 Primary zone** (City/Nexus/Hive) -- supports 2 specializations
- **Up to 3 Secondary zones** (Generator, Mining, Agriculture) -- each supports 1 specialization

Key specializations:
- **Heavy Industry**: 100 Metallurgists (dedicated forge world)
- **Research Enclave**: 30 Physicists + 30 Biologists + 30 Engineers
- **Mixed Industry**: 50 Metallurgists + 50 Artisans (generalist)

Specialization multipliers: Standard x1, Habitat x1.5, Ecumenopolis/Hive/Machine x3, Ring World x5. Secondary districts get x2 (stacks).

**Critical in 4.3**: Strategic resource deposits give much less without matching specializations. Research the gateway techs early (Global Energy Management, Mineral Purification, Food Processing).

### Capital Specializations
The capital can be specialized into: Forge Capital, Factory Capital, Capital Extraction World, or Trade Capital. There is NO "Capital Mining World" or "Capital Research World".

### Habitat Designations
Foundry/Factory/Mining/Research/Trade/Energy/Farming/Fortress Station, plus Refinery Station.

### Ecumenopolis Designations
Ecumenopolis Foundry, Factory, Industrial, Research.

### Ring World Designations
Agriculture/Generator/Research/Commercial Ring World.

Planetary Ascension: +10% per tier in 4.3 (was +25%). Pour Unity into highest-value planets first (forge worlds, tech worlds, capital).

## Efficiency Buildings Are Critical

Always build efficiency buildings before adding more raw production:
- Energy Nexus: +2 energy per Technician
- Mineral Purification Plant: +1 minerals per Miner
- Alloy Mega-Forge: +1 alloys per Metallurgist
- Civilian Repli-Complex: +2 consumer goods per Artisan

## Pop Management (4.3 Changes)

- Unemployed pops now demote to civilian at month end (immediate demotion)
- Pop growth nerfed: Fertile 30%->20%, Vat Grown 33%->25%
- Avoid migration treaties early -- they scatter pops instead of concentrating them
- Population is your ultimate bottleneck. Never build what you cannot staff.

## Trade System

Trade is now a basic material resource used for ship upkeep, colony upkeep, market purchases, and branch offices (4.3: costs trade instead of energy).

- Offworld Trading Company: +4 trade per Trade Hub (doubled in 4.3)
- Maximum collection range: 7 jumps (6 Trade Hubs + Hyperlane Registrar)
- Gateway networks are the safest long-distance trade approach

## Mid-Game Transitions (2250-2350)

1. Shift planets to specialized designations
2. Push planetary ascension tiers on best planets
3. Begin megastructure preparation (Mega Engineering + Master Builders)
4. Specialize districts for strategic resources

## Late-Game Mega-Economy

Megastructure priority:
1. Dyson Sphere: 1000 energy/month
2. Matter Decompressor: massive minerals
3. Science Nexus: large research boost
4. Ring Worlds: x5 specialization multiplier -- best planets in the game

## Common Mistakes

1. Building ahead of population (the #1 mistake)
2. Running generalist planets instead of specializing
3. Ignoring efficiency buildings
4. Mineral neglect (minerals feed everything)
5. Over-investing in Unity (base increased to 10/month in 4.3)
6. Expecting 4.0-4.2 power levels -- Cetus deliberately reduced everything
